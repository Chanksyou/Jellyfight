# Jelly Fight

A third-person game where you play a ~9 cm jellyfish of your own design inside a real-scale 3D model of the apartment.

## Run it

## The game

You're an immortal jellyfish growing up over one night in the apartment. Stage 1 (Polyp) is the living room and its kitchen strip, midnight to 2 am (4:20 of real time before the boss comes).

It's night: the room is lit only by its lamps (warm pools of light, dark corners, a faint haze across the room), and you're a fluorescent jelly in it: a tall clear bell with glowing canals and a bright rim, long glowing tentacles and frilly blue oral arms, with a soft halo and a small light of your own that tints the floor around you. The bugs, elites and the Vacuum carry a faint glow and a warm rim light so they read against the dark, and their angry eyes shine. That's act 1; act 2 is the hallway and the bathroom (below).

- **You attack on your own, two ways.** Your main attack is **bubbles**: the bell blows a steady stream of bubbles, one after another (about 1.8 a second to start), at the nearest enemy in range; each pops hard (6 damage) with a small splash. Fire-rate cards make the stream faster. Each extra bubble (a +1 Bubble card, Bubble Wand, Ephyra Bell) adds one more bubble to every blow, side by side and flying parallel, but costs damage: every bubble does 25% less per extra one, compounding (2 bubbles do 75% each, 3 do 56%, 6 do 24%), so more bubbles cover a wider lane for less punch each. Level-up cards improve them (bubbles, range, bubble damage, fire rate, bubble size; the Bubbles card is always Epic, +1; every card's Common/Rare/Epic amounts are `CARD_VALUES` in `src/stats.js`). Element treasures fire independently alongside bubbles: fire (burns), lightning (arcs to 2 more), ice (slows; repeated hits refresh the slow), acid (puddles), wind (winds up and fires half as often as bubbles; scenery collisions deal its hit damage again) and glitter (bigger splash). Each element changes the projectile itself so you can see it coming: a fireball trailing flames, a crackling ball of lightning, a spinning ice shard, a dripping acid glob, a crescent wind blade, a glitter bomb shedding sparks (each owned Element has its own timer). Up close, your **tentacles** sting automatically; the Stinging Frills evolution improves them, and so do their own treasures (Spaghetti Strand, Tape Measure, Chopsticks, Stinging Nettle, Paperclip Chain). You swim, jump and position.

Four yellow **rubber ducks** sit round the middle of the tub floor (hop on them). At 75% and again at 25% of its Health comes its **acid phase**: the spout pours green acid until the tub is half full, floating in it costs 2 Health a second, and the ducks bob up on it. The Clog floats at the head of the tub, where the bar says SHIELDED and it can't be hurt, and lobs a hair chunk at three of the four ducks every 1.5 s, each duck showing a circle and half sphere until its chunk lands (3, and you're thrown off): get on the duck it spares, never the same one twice running. After eight rounds the acid drains and the fight goes on. The second time the acid swirls: the ducks circle the middle, carrying you with them, and each chunk is aimed where its duck will be when it lands. Below 65% it's angry: faster, shorter pauses, double lashes, and a spinning **gunk spray** in three streams of Shots at once (2 a glob; they alternate floor level, which you jump, and jump height, which pass over you if you stay down). Its Warnings are a sickly yellow-green. (`src/clog.js`.) Beat it and you grow again, into a young Medusa, and the end screen offers **On to act 3 →** the same way. The act lives in `src/stage2.js`; `src/stages.js` lists the acts and carries the run between them (sessionStorage, reloading the page).
- **Act 3: the bedroom.** Beat the Clog and the run carries on into the bedroom, 4 am to 6 am (4:20 again). Dragonflies, mantises, stag beetles and wasp nests defend its furniture. When time runs out, **The Great White One** takes over the bed: an animated plush shark that walks on fin arms and dives through the quilt. Its attacks include a lunging jaw snap, leap, bite/fin/tail combo, tail spin, rear bite, four consecutive eruptions, a burrowed bite, slowing maelstrom, and three fish-flinging tornadoes below 85% Health. Confirmed melee hits mark Prey and bleed; bites start Feeding Frenzy. Every attack has a finite-height warning. Piranha pools expire and cap at six. Dendi finishes it at zero Health, followed by the evolution, Legendary reward and Act 4. See [the model and fight notes](docs/shark-boss.md).

**Music:** an adaptive soundtrack synthesized in the browser (`src/music.js`), in the spirit of Risk of Rain 2. Exploring plays "Puddle Drift", inspired by Daft Punk's Tron: Legacy score: dark string pads, then a pulsing sequenced analog bass that carries the song, a slow arpeggio, deep half-time drums and toms, and a low brass lead as more bugs close in and the night gets later, with no bright highs to distract; the Vacuum fight plays "Domestic Machinery", after deadmau5's "HR 8938 Cephei" but dubbier: pumping supersaw chords, a wobbling reese bass that speeds up as the Vacuum weakens, risers into every phrase, and a supersaw lead once it's below about 60% health; its suction drags the whole mix through a dub filter. The Vacuum has its own sounds for everything it does: a boot-up chime, an alarm and a roar for the charge and a clanging crash into the wall, a wobbling dub roar for the suction, whirring brushes and swishing sweeps, a clunk and skittering for the roach dump, a whirling siren for the spin, a wail and four pops for the lanternflies, a shimmering shield that pings when hit and shatters, an angry siren as it enrages at half health, and a long power-down when it dies. The pause menu muffles it; 🎵 turns it off (M mutes everything).

**Getting around:** the jelly swims at a steady speed while its bell pulses in strokes (`src/swim.js`), faster the harder you push. It has a single jump, 20 cm high (`jumpHeight` in `src/config.js`: enough to jump out of the bathroom sink bowl, 17.5 cm deep); each Pen Spring treasure adds a jump in mid-air (take up to 3 for a quadruple jump). Floor vents fling you in an arc straight onto the sofa, coffee table, media console, desk, lounge chair and stovetop. Use the sofa vent to reach its seat.

## Changing the game

Most changes are edits to plain-text files, not code. `CLAUDE.md` is the full map (agents read it automatically):

- **`content/enemies.kdl`**: every bug as a few numbers and a few behaviour words, e.g. `chase 0.3`, or `spit every=2.6 dmg=2 aim=0.7 shot="pin" still=true`. Each word is defined once in `src/words.js` with what its numbers mean. A typo stops loading with a message naming the file and line.
- **`content/waves.kdl`**: how fast bugs arrive, how many at once (living bugs only: the elites and the boss don't take a place), how much tougher they get, and from when each kind appears.
- **`content/treasures.kdl`**: all 102 treasures, each a name, icon, text and effect words (and `stack=N` for stackable ones, or `level 2 text="…" { … }` / `level 3 …` blocks for ones that level up), e.g. `pierce 3`, `crit chance=0.2 mult=3`, or `every 5 { ring 0.13 dmg=1 push=0.035 }`. A new treasure that combines existing effects needs no code.
- **`content/look.css`**: colours and render numbers: the night fill, haze, bloom, the jelly's size, colour, glow and light, how much enemies glow, gut colours, the camera, and how often and how sharp the game draws (frame-rate cap, pixel ratio range, how many lamps a phone lights at once).

**Stories** (`src/stories/<area>.js`, shared helpers in `src/stories/kit.js`, loaded in order by `src/stories.js`) are named situations in the real game, like Storybook: `index.html?story=words/spit-pin` opens one live (on a phone too), `index.html?stories` lists them all. Each story's `play()` also runs headlessly and checks what should happen. `node tests/run.mjs` plays every story plus a phone touch check and fails on any broken behaviour or console error. Setup: `cd tests && npm install && npx playwright install chromium`.

**The balance bot** (`node tests/balance.mjs 5`) plays whole nights on autopilot and reports how long it lasted, its level and kills, whether it beat the boss, and which sources took its Health. Run it before and after anything that could make the game harder or easier.

**The matchup test** (`BUGS=roach node tests/matchup.mjs`) puts every treasure (at level 1 and its top level), every Epic level-up card and every evolution against one big bug and a chasing clump of ten, and reports how much faster each clears them than nothing does. Leave out `BUGS=` to try every bug. Use it to spot outliers; rate a single treasure with `tests/clear.mjs`.

## Dev: layout editor

**Dev tools** sit behind one **🔒 Dev** button on the main screen: the password (`chan`, in `src/main.js`; it keeps the tools out of players' way, it isn't security) shows 🛠 Layout, 👹 Fight boss, 🐞 1 on 1, 🧪 Test kit, ⚖️ Tuner and 🩺 Diagnostics. They stay open for the tab (sessionStorage).

**👹 Fight boss** lists every act's boss; pick one and a fresh run goes straight to its intro (level 1, no treasures), for testing the fight. A boss from another act reloads the game into that act first, like a 1 on 1 elite pick. (There is no generic "other act" jump any more — with more than two acts it stopped meaning anything; the Layout editor and the pickers are how you get to another act.)

**🧪 Test kit** (`src/test-kit.js`) tries any treasure or evolution on the run in front of you: search or filter by rarity, **+** adds a copy (or the next level, or an evolution), **−** takes one away, and the row up top shows what you have. **New test run** starts a clean one with the waves off and you unable to die (both are toggles); **Spawn 10 roaches** or **a mixed pack** of this act's bugs puts something in front of you to try it on. The game holds still while it's open; **▶ Play with this** carries on.

**🐞 1 on 1** lists every enemy (each bug, plus every act's elites; picking an elite from another act reloads the game into that act first): pick one and a fresh run starts with just it, no waves, scheduled treasures or boss. A bug turns up near you and another comes 1.5 s after you clear it; an elite puts you on its high ground and comes back 4 s after you beat it. Pause to pick another; Restart goes back to a normal run (`Run.startDuel` in `src/run.js`).

**🛠 Layout** (dev tools) lets you move, rotate, raise/lower, hide and delete the furniture and objects. Tap or click an object to select it (a yellow box shows it), or pick it from the toolbar's list — hidden and deleted objects stay in the list so they can be brought back (⌂ puts one back, 👁 un-hides or un-deletes). Drag a selected object to move it. Desktop: WASD pan, wheel zoom, right-drag turns the view, Q/E rotate 15° (Shift: 90°), R/F raise/lower 1 cm (Shift: 5 cm), H hide, Del delete, Ctrl+Z undo. Touch: drag empty space to pan, pinch to zoom, and use the toolbar.

Edits are saved in the browser (`localStorage` key `jf-layout-v1`, one slice per act) and applied on every load before collisions are built, so moved furniture is solid where it is. The **Act** buttons in the top bar switch which act you're editing — one per act in the game, so a new act gets one automatically — the game reloads into that act with the editor open, and each act's edits are kept and exported separately, so an edit can never leak into another act's rooms. **Export** shows a JSON code tagged with its act; paste it back to Claude to bake the layout into the stage for everyone. Baked edits live per act in `src/layout-baked.js`; they load first and become each object's "home", with your own browser edits on top. Hide just stops showing an object; **delete** (🗑) also records it as `deleted` in the export, so the bake drops it for good. **Vents** are edited here too: each shows an orange disc (the grille on the floor) and a cyan ball (where it throws you) joined by the jump's arc. Drag either and it sticks to whatever surface is under the pointer (point at a cushion or a shelf); R/F nudge it up or down, ⌂ puts it back. They export with the furniture as `vent:<name>` entries, which get baked into the vent list in `src/stage1.js`. Other stage markers (treasure spots, elites) don't follow moved furniture yet. Code: `src/layout.js`.

## Hosting on Cloudflare (public leaderboard)

The game also runs as a Cloudflare Worker named `jelly-fight` (`wrangler.jsonc`). The Worker serves the game's files from Cloudflare's CDN and answers `api/scores` (`worker/index.js`) from a D1 database called `jelly-fight-scores`, so anyone can post a score: signed in or not, any organization. On the game's own site, `src/leaderboard.js` uses that; on claude.ai it still uses the artifact's database.

- **Deploys:** Cloudflare Workers Builds, connected to this GitHub repo (`Chanksyou/Jellyfight`; the Cloudflare GitHub app needs access to it). Don't use the dashboard's "Import a repository": it makes a separate copy that never gets updates. Every push to `main` redeploys. Build command: none. Deploy command: `npx wrangler deploy`. Wrangler creates the D1 database on the first deploy (no ID to paste), and the Worker creates its tables on the first request.
- **What's served:** the repo root, minus what `.assetsignore` lists (`tests/`, `tools/`, `worker/`, the config, the docs, and the unused whole-apartment models).
- **The board's rules:** a player is a random id kept in their browser, and the board only ever sends back your own (an id is all it takes to post as that player). Scores are capped at 60,000, names are trimmed to 20 characters with HTML stripped, and each player can post once every 15 s and each address once every 5 s (addresses are stored only as a salted hash; set an `IP_SALT` secret on the Worker to choose your own salt). Scores come from the player's browser, so this stops casual cheating, not a determined cheater.
- **Try it locally:** `npx wrangler dev`, then open http://localhost:8787.

## How it fits together

The apartment is a static 3D model, baked to `assets/apartment.glb` (geometry, materials, textures, and doors as separate nodes), plus `assets/apartment.json` (floor plan, acts, door angles, the lights at midnight). The game doesn't load that whole file: `tools/split.mjs` cuts it into one file per act, and each act loads only its own rooms. `apartment-act1.glb` (~3.6 MB) is the living room and the hallway you can see down from it (seen only: an invisible wall keeps you out of it in act 1), plus every room's walls, floors and doors, so every view is solid; `apartment-act2.glb` (~1.1 MB) the bathroom and hall closets; `apartment-act3.glb` (~0.9 MB) the bedroom. A stage lists the parts it needs (`parts` in `src/stage1.js`); `GAME.loadRooms(['act2'])` brings in more at the start of a later act (solid, with layout edits, on the minimap, with that act's lamps). Each part is meshopt-compressed (needs WebAssembly) with a `-q` twin used automatically if WebAssembly is blocked. Nothing from the original apartment app runs in the game. A few baked pieces that read as flat cutouts at jelly height are replaced at load by detailed models built to their measured boxes (`src/set-dressing.js`): the wall TV (a glossy near-frameless panel on its mount) and its soundbar, and the shower curtain. On a phone the baked glass (the coffee and side table tops) is softened at load instead (`softenGlass` in `src/apartment.js`): no clearcoat, roughness at least 0.15 — the clearcoat's second specular lobe against every lamp was the frame-rate cliff when the table filled the view.

`src/boot.js` sets up three.js (r186 from jsDelivr), loads the apartment with `src/apartment.js`, then starts `src/main.js`.

The apartment's colors and lights were authored for three.js r128, so the game keeps color management off and `src/legacy-lighting.js` renders the lights with r128's falloff and scaling.

The camera can sit 2 mm from a wall without flicker thanks to a reversed depth buffer (`src/boot.js`). Where the GPU lacks it, desktop falls back to a logarithmic depth buffer and phones (log depth defeats their GPUs' hidden-surface removal) to a 5 mm near plane.

### Re-baking the apartment

`tools/apartment-source.html` is the full apartment app, kept only as the source for the bake. After changing it:

```
cd tools
npm install
npx playwright install chromium
npm run export
```

Where jsDelivr is blocked or Playwright's Chromium isn't installed (a cloud session), point the bake at a local three.js 0.170.0 (`npm pack three@0.170.0`, unpacked) and the machine's Chromium: `CHROMIUM=/opt/pw-browsers/chromium_headless_shell-1194/chrome-linux/headless_shell THREE_LOCAL=/path/to/three/package node export.mjs`.

The bathroom's close-up details live in the source too: the vanity's undermount basin (a real bowl under a hole in the counter), the oval toilet seat, the loop-pile bath mat, canvas laundry bags, panelled doors and fabric coats. A material whose bump map is its own color map keeps its bump through the bake.

The exporter (`tools/exporter.js`) keeps the meshes inside the rooms. It drops everything else: the neighborhood, the corridor, the sky, Dendi (the game's cat is built at runtime instead — see Characters above), particles and helpers. Doors are exported closed as `door-<id>` nodes. `tools/compress.mjs` then meshopt-compresses the geometry (about 17 MB down to 5 MB).

## Characters and swapping in a real model

A look is plain JSON (`{ name, body, finish, color, accent, pattern, eyes, eyeColor, mouth, top, topColor, feet, size }`). `buildCharacter(look, heightMeters)` returns an avatar:

```js
{ root: THREE.Object3D, update(dt, { speed, walkSpeed, grounded, vy, swim, vel }), land(impact), dispose(),
  pulse(), tentacles }   // optional: a jump's bell squeeze; a TentacleRig the Lash strikes with
```

The player only talks to that interface (`player.setAvatar(avatar)`), so a rigged glTF character can replace the procedural one by writing another builder that returns the same shape. It would drive its animation mixer from `update()` and map the look's colors and hat onto the model.

**Dendi the cat** (`src/cat.js`) is a procedural model in his own right: one skinned mesh (26 bones) built and painted from photos of the real cat — brown mackerel tabby, bold black dorsal stripe, M forehead, green eyes, black bow collar — with procedural poses (idle, curious, walk, trot, sit, loaf, stretch, and a paw smack). He is never seen during a run: he exists hidden in every act, and when a boss's Health reaches zero the fight freezes, the screen fades to black, and he is standing beside the boss when it fades back (on the bed beside the shark in act 3) — one smack of his paw starts the death animation, and then he is gone again. While he's there the camera moves to a view of its own, side-on to him and the boss with the jelly in front, from inside the arena with nothing in the way (looking down into the bathtub for the Clog; `--cat-scene-*` in look.css), and eases back afterwards. The bosses only go *doomed* on their own (`dying` in `src/vacuum.js` / `src/clog.js` / `src/shark.js`); the scene in `src/main.js` calls `die()` at the moment the paw lands.
