# Jelly Fight

A third-person game where you play a ~9 cm jellyfish of your own design inside a real-scale 3D model of the apartment.

## Run it

ES modules won't load over `file://`, so serve the folder:

```
npx http-server -c-1        # or: python -m http.server
```

Open the printed localhost URL. The VS Code "Live Server" extension also works.

## The game

You're an immortal jellyfish growing up over one night in the apartment. Stage 1 (Polyp) is the living room and its kitchen strip, midnight to 2 am (4:20 of real time before the boss comes).

It's night: the room is lit only by its lamps (warm pools of light, dark corners, a faint haze across the room), and you're a fluorescent jelly in it: a tall clear bell with glowing canals and a bright rim, long glowing tentacles and frilly blue oral arms, with a soft halo and a small light of your own that tints the floor around you. The bugs, elites and the Vacuum carry a faint glow and a warm rim light so they read against the dark, and their angry eyes shine. That's act 1; act 2 is the hallway and the bathroom (below).

- **You attack on your own, two ways.** Your main attack is **bubbles**: the bell blows a steady stream of bubbles, one after another (about 1.8 a second to start), at the nearest enemy in range; each pops hard (6 damage) with a small splash. Fire-rate cards make the stream faster. Each extra bubble (a +1 Bubble card, Bubble Wand, Ephyra Bell) adds one more bubble to every blow, side by side and flying parallel, but costs damage: every bubble does 25% less per extra one, compounding (2 bubbles do 75% each, 3 do 56%, 6 do 24%), so more bubbles cover a wider lane for less punch each. Level-up cards improve them (bubbles, range, bubble damage, fire rate, bubble size; the Bubbles card is always Epic, +1; every card's Common/Rare/Epic amounts are `CARD_VALUES` in `src/stats.js`). Element treasures infuse them, and they stack: fire (burns; shatters frozen enemies), lightning (arcs to 2 more), ice (chills, then freezes), acid (puddles), wind (a crescent blade that knocks back) and glitter (bigger splash). Each element changes the projectile itself so you can see it coming: a fireball trailing flames, a crackling ball of lightning, a spinning ice shard, a dripping acid glob, a gust of spinning wind rings, a glitter bomb shedding sparks (with several elements, bubbles take turns). Up close, your **tentacles** sting automatically; the Stinging Frills evolution improves them, and so do their own treasures (Spaghetti Strand, Tape Measure, Chopsticks, Stinging Nettle, Paperclip Chain). You swim, jump and position.
- **Bugs** come for you and drop **XP** when you clear them (every 5 XP comes as one big, brighter drop, the rest as small ones): **cockroaches** scuttle straight at you (a little slower than you swim, so you can always get away from one); **ant squads** (five ants in a block, from 0:30) curl into a ball and roll into you; **mosquitoes** hover out of reach and shoot laser bolts: a flickering red beam shows where the bolt will go (brighter as the shot nears), it locks on halfway through, and stepping off the line dodges it. From 2:00, **spotted lanternflies** crawl after you, crouch over a red ring marking where they'll land, then leap high (flashing their red hindwings) and slam down on it: step out of the ring. There aren't many at once, but each is worth a lot. In act 2, **giant house spiders** (from 0:45) stalk after you on eight long, hairy legs (a real spider's gait: two alternating sets of four) but keep their distance, stopping about 55 cm away and backing off if you close in; from there one rears up, front legs raised, and lobs a ball of web that arcs down onto where you were: it stings (2) and webs you, slowing your swimming for 1.5 s. Keep moving and it lands behind you. Also in act 2, **house flies** (from 0:30) buzz in close beside you, hang there rubbing their front legs and draw back while a short orange line on the floor marks the strike (it locks halfway through), then poke you with their straw of a mouth for 3: step off the line or back out of reach. And from 1:00 in act 2, **giant millipedes** (glossy olive-black rings banded orange, rows of rippling orange legs) slither after you like snakes, swinging round in arcs (their bodies never fold through themselves). Up close one curls into an armoured ball standing on its edge (hits mostly glance off it: 75% less damage), revs up spinning in place while its lane lights up amber on the floor (the aim locks halfway through), then rolls down it at you for 4. Get out of the lane, and hit it once it uncurls. From 1:30 in act 2, **ladybugs** (glossy clear-coated seven-spot ladybirds) fly circles round you with their wings a blur, then land, lift their red elytra high to show a missile on a rail on their back, and lock on: a red reticle closes in round you, beeping faster. Then the missile launches (a flash, a smoke burst, an ignition roar) and hunts you for 4 s, trailing smoke, turning only so fast, so outrunning it or breaking hard across its path makes it overshoot. If it catches you it explodes (fireball, sparks, a shockwave and a shake) for 3; out of fuel it coughs, sputters and falls trailing black smoke, clinks onto the floor and fizzles away. Hit a ladybug while it's grounded and locking on. Each piece of high ground has an **elite**, an object there that came alive, each with two attacks it alternates (elites rest 1.4x quicker between attacks and play them 1.15x faster: `RECOVER`, `TEMPO` in `src/elites.js`; act 1 health: Controller 195, Mug 220, Kettle 275), every one telegraphed on the ground before it lands: the **Controller** on the media console (three colored aim lines, then a button barrage; a red rumble circle that fills, then a shockwave), the **Mug** on the desk (coffee lobs onto orange circles, each with a see-through half dome over it filling from the floor up: the coffee lands as a splash that fills the dome, 2 to anything of the jelly inside it, so jump it or step out, and leaves a scalding puddle; a tip-over with an arrow, then a spill that pours puddles along it) and the **Kettle** on the stove (whistles with a yellow steam wedge on the counter under its spout and a see-through cone of steam over it, rising as it spreads to about 1.5x its half-width at the far end, both filling from the floor up: then it blasts, 2 to anything of the jelly inside the cone, so jumping doesn't clear it but stepping out of it does; boils over, raining drops on filling orange circles, each with a half dome over it: each drop lands as a small splash, 2 to anything of the jelly inside its dome, so jump it or step out). They turn to face you before winding up and only fight when you come close. Beat one for 20 XP, and it leaves a treasure (a golden chest) where it stood; elite bugs leave one where they fall too.
- **HUD:** top left, your level in a big gold ring, a wide Health bar with its number (red when low), a gold XP bar under it and your evolutions and treasures, big enough to read. One that goes off on a timer or has a cooldown wears a clock face (`Run.cooldowns`, `Hud.setCooldowns`): greyed with a dark wedge for the wait still to go, sweeping away clockwise, it lights up with a cyan ring when it's ready and flashes when it goes off; a once-a-run one (Snooze Button, Immortal Jelly's rebirth) greys out for good once used. Top middle, the boss countdown ("Boss in 4:19"), with the night's clock and the boss's name small under it. The minimap sits bottom right (top right on phones). While an elite fights you, its name and health show under the clock (gold), and every elite has a big outlined health bar over it, green to red as it's worn down. Marked enemies (Sticky Note, acid puddles: they take 50% more damage) show a little skull with red eyes over them.
- **Treasures in the room:** five a run, at 0:15, 1:00, 1:45, 2:45 and 3:45, a treasure turns up somewhere else in the room as a small golden chest (glowing softly and twinkling, and on the minimap) and waits 22 s, its ring on the floor draining and blinking as time runs out. Reach it in time for a treasure pick; otherwise it fades away. A beaten elite leaves one too, near where it fell on the nearest open spot (nothing over it, inside it or crowding it up to the jelly's height, so never inside or under furniture: `Run.openSpotNear`; the story `elites/every-chest-reachable` walks to every elite's chest in both acts), and that one waits for the rest of the act. A treasure never sits on a vent's landing point: a spot that close moves over 15-35 cm on the same surface. The times and how long each stays are `treasures` in `src/stage1.js`.
- **A player's first run** introduces the game one thing at a time (`src/first-run.js`), in this order: first just swimming among roaches while the bubbles fire on their own (no Element pick, no minimap); on the first hit, one plain line for a few seconds, "That's your Health, top left. Run out and the run ends."; the first Level-up's cards; the Element pick straight after that first card; then the first Treasure at the next scheduled time after the Element pick (earlier scheduled times are skipped; an Elite's Treasure still drops), with the minimap appearing alongside it. A player is new until a run of theirs ends (dried out or the boss beaten) and has no saved best score (the leaderboard's player id can't tell: it's made on the first load). Act 2 and the dev modes (1 on 1, fight the boss) are never a first run, and a dev run doesn't count as played. The stories `first-run/the-first-minute` and `phone/the-first-minute` play the whole sequence.
- **Element attacks:** every run starts with a Legendary pick of 1 of 3 elements (fire, lightning, ice, acid, wind or glitter); the elements are Legendary treasures. Beating a boss (after the metamorphosis) gives the **Boss reward**: a Legendary pick of three that always offers at least one element you don't have yet, while any are left. Each element is an attack of its own: it fires its own projectile on its own timer (a fireball, ball lightning, an ice shard, an acid glob, a wind gust, a glitter bomb), separate from your bubbles, which stay plain bubbles. Your Bubble damage, Fire rate, Range, Bubble size and extra Bubbles all apply to element attacks too (crits and marks as well). Each element has **5 upgrades** (30 in all, e.g. Lighter Fluid: fire burns 50% hotter; 9V Battery: lightning arcs to 2 more; Snow Globe: ice chills the whole burst; Pinwheel: one more gust a shot), never in an ordinary treasure pick: every 3rd treasure you open offers one of them (for an element you own) among its three. Base elements never come from treasures in the room. The numbers: `ELEMENT_BASE` in `src/words.js` (raised by the `element-up` word) and `ELEMENT` in `src/bubbles.js`.
- **Level up** by collecting XP (cockroaches drop 1, ant squads 2, mosquitoes 5; each level needs 1.45x the XP of the last, starting at 3: 3, 4, 6, 9, 13, 19, 28…): pick 1 of 3 cards, each one stat and one number (bubbles, range, bubble damage, fire rate, Health, move speed, Health regen, dodge %, luck). Regen, dodge and luck start at 0; dodge caps at 60%. Luck makes rarer cards and treasures come up more often. One free reroll per level.
- **The minimap** is round, centred on you and turned so up is where the camera looks (the way forward moves you), slightly see-through. It's kept quiet: the room in one tone with faint footprints of the big furniture, you as a cyan arrow (the way the jelly faces), small red dots for bugs, and coloured dots for what's worth going to (gold treasures, orange elites, red boss), with a tiny ▲ / ▼ when one is well above or below you. A treasure or elite further away than the map shows waits on its rim, in its direction.
- **Treasures** come from treasures in the room (the golden chests), beaten elites' chests, the starting pick and the Boss reward: pick 1 of 3 to keep all run, each **Common, Rare, Epic or Legendary**. Where a pick comes from decides what it offers: a chest on the schedule offers Common and Rare, an elite's chest Rare and Epic, the Boss reward only Legendary (luck shifts the odds within those). **Stacking:** a treasure without levels can be taken again and adds up (3 copies unless it says otherwise, at most 5; ×2 in the HUD); rule-changers and every Legendary are one of a kind. There are 105. The newer kinds: **converters** that grow one stat with another (Medicine Ball: Bubble damage per 10 max Health; Roller Skate, Paperclip Chain, Piggy Bank, Spare Change, Tea Bag), **conditionals** (Dry Sock in the air, Hot Water Bottle under 30% Health), **on-hurt** ones (Windowsill Cactus; Toaster Cord, where a tentacle lashes back at the bug that hit you), **procs** (Static Balloon, Thumbtack, Fly Swatter), kill growers with a cap (Dustpan) and Health crumbs (Meatball, Leftover). A tentacle line (Spaghetti Strand +1 Tentacle, Tape Measure reach, Chopsticks speed, Stinging Nettle damage), Bike Pump (Bubble size) and Spring Insole (Jump height) raise stats nothing else did; Wind-Up Key brings about 20% more treasures into the room and Can Opener hits Elites and the Boss harder. **Legendary** (14): the six elements, Hand Mirror (reflects a hit every 15 s), Snooze Button (once a run, a lethal hit leaves you at half), Disco Ball (bubbles echo to the next bug for 65% damage), Bubble Bath (a ring of 8 bubbles every 5 s), Hot Water Bottle, Power Bank (every element fires 50% faster), Cat Toy Mouse (a wind-up mouse that hits the nearest bug for 30 every 10 s) and Notebook (4 cards a Level). The rest: the six elements and their 30 upgrades, bubble upgrades, stat boosts (regen, dodge, damage, range), Brotato-style trade-offs (a glass cannon, life steal, bait that brings more bugs and more XP) and slow growers (+max Health or damage every N kills), things that attack on their own (a chord every 5 s, remote zaps, orbiting fairy lights, a burning moonbeam, Lego traps that burst, big glowing bowling marbles, slowing cold snaps, auras), and rule-benders (marks that make enemies take more damage, more XP, better or more level-up cards, slower bugs). **Levels:** the treasures with a trick of their own whose numbers can grow have three levels: Bobby Pin, Gold Ring, Reed Stick, Sticky Note, Cotton Ball, Rubber Duck, Whale, Baby Bottle, Bath Salt, Guitar Pick, Bath Bomb, Ice Cube, TV Remote, Lego Brick, Marble, Fairy Lights, Magnifying Glass, Glow Stick, Fridge Magnet and Houseplant. You can only be offered the level after the one you have; the card says **Lv 2** or **Lv 3** and what it adds. A critical hit shows a bright star-flash on the bug, and a slowed bug a turning frost ring under it (`--crit`, `--slowed` in `content/look.css`).
- **The Vacuum** comes when the timer runs out: a robot vacuum that powers on in front of the kitchen. It chases and bumps you, and cycles through attacks: a telegraphed charge, suction that pulls you in, spinning-brush sweeps, dumping cockroaches from its bin, launching 4 lanternflies out of its bin in high arcs (rings mark where they land) and raising a purple shield that makes it immune until all 4 are dead (tethers show which flies hold it up; the boss bar turns purple and says SHIELDED; twice a fight at most), and (once hurt) a spin that sprays dust. At half health it **enrages**: it stops and shudders, rearing up with its light strobing red and sparks flying (ENRAGED!), then does everything twice as fast. When any boss goes down, all the XP in the arena (its own and its summons') flies to you.
- Beat it and you **metamorphose**: pick 1 of 3 **evolutions** that change how you play for the rest of the run (one per act's boss, never the same twice; `content/evolutions.kdl`). **Box Jelly**: 4 long tentacles that stack burning venom, bubbles 40% slower. **Man o' War**: two polyps circle you and shoot with you at half damage, −20% Health. **Immortal Jelly**: once an act a killing blow makes you an untouchable polyp for 5 s, then you bloom back whole; −25% Health and no regen. **Moon Jelly**: a pulse every 2.5 s that stings, knocks back and surges you forward; bubbles reach 30% less far. **Lion's Mane**: +60% Health and bugs touching you get stung; −15% Move speed. **Upside-down Jelly**: stand still a second to root for +80% Fire rate, +1 Health/s and +1 pierce. Each adds its own piece to the jelly (venom strands, a sail crest and polyps, a gold halo, a moon ring, a mane, fronds on the floor; `src/adornments.js`) on top of the player's own design, which never changes. Dry out and you shrink back to a polyp and start over.
- **Score and leaderboard:** every run ends with one combined score: 10 per bug cleared, +50 per golden elite bug, +300 per high-ground elite, 100 per level above 1, 150 per treasure, 2 per second survived before the boss (up to 520), up to 1,500 for damage dealt to the Vacuum, and +3,000 for beating it plus 20 for every second under 3 minutes (`Run.scoreParts` in `src/run.js`). The end screen asks for a name (empty = Guest; the last one you typed is remembered) and posts the score only when you press **📤 Submit score** (finishing act 1 doesn't post; the run's score goes up at its end). On the game's claude.ai page the score goes on a shared leaderboard (🏆 in the pause menu and on the end screen): one entry per player, their best run, top 10 shown (`src/leaderboard.js`, the artifact's `db`). Opening the board reads the table there and then and redraws as scores come in, so it never depends on the live feed having delivered; your own just-posted score always shows. The end screen also has **🏠 Main menu** (from a later act it reloads into act 1's menu). Who can post: anyone signed in to claude.ai in the page owner's organization who opens it by link, or anyone shared in as Contributor or above. Each player writes only their own entry (the page's `db` rules: `scores` read `view`, write `owner`; `scores/{self}` write `interact`). Signed-in visitors from outside the organization get view-only access from claude.ai, so they see the board but can't post: the end screen says "view-only here, not posted" and their best stays in the browser. Anywhere else (signed out, a saved copy) the game keeps your best in the browser.
- **4:20 per stage.** Then the boss comes: grab what treasures you can before it does (both acts; `duration` in each stage file).
- **Act 2: the hallway and the bathroom.** Beat the Vacuum and grow, and the end screen offers **On to act 2 →**: the page reloads straight into the hallway, no main menu (just "Act 2" while it loads; a phone starts on its own, desktop takes one click to lock the mouse), with everything the run had (level, stats, treasures, score so far, which also goes on the leaderboard). You're much stronger by now, so act 2 pushes back: bugs have 2x health (and still toughen with the hour), the elites 1.8x, the Clog 1.6x, and every hit you take does 1.5x (`toughness`, `eliteHp`, `bossHp`, `power` in `src/stage2.js`). It also sends a little fewer bugs (0.85x the spawn rate and how many at once, `bugs`) and each drops a little more XP (1.2x, `xpMult`). Its waves are four bugs, joining in turn: **house flies** from the start, **spiders** from 0:30, **millipedes** from 1:15 and **ladybugs** from 2:00. Five vents fling you onto the vanity, the toilet lid, the sideboard, the bench and the recycling bin, and treasures turn up in 14 spots across both rooms (bath mat, rugs, vanity, inside the sink, toilet lid, the hall runner, the sideboard, the bench…). The laundry and coat closets stand ajar so you can peek in, but a force field keeps you out (and out of the living room). Every invisible wall shows as a glowing hex force field, brightest at floor level and rippling when you swim up to it (`src/forcefield.js`); act 1's wall into the hall and both bosses' arena walls (while the fight is on) have one too. The vanity has an elite: **the Soap Dispenser** (the glass pump bottle by the tap, standing in for the real one; the vanity vent lands you beside it). Its pink soap drains as you hurt it, so the level is its health. It alternates a **soap squirt** (three pump strokes, each lobbing a glob onto a pink circle filling where you stand: 2 damage, and the glob leaves a slick that takes your grip away so you slide) and a **bubble ring** (it foams up while a pink ring fills around it, then lets go a ring of soap bubbles drifting outward with one gap to slip through, 2 each). Under half health it's angry: quicker, four squirts, and a second ring right after the first with the gap elsewhere. Beat it for a treasure pick. The hallway's ornate bronze wall clock over the cubby bench (the one with the tennis ball) is an elite too: **the Wall Clock**, modelled in detail after the real one (Victory in an arched niche raising a laurel wreath and holding a herald's horn, a dial with gilt Roman numerals and pierced gilt hands in a laurel garland, a bearded mask with acanthus wings, a lattice pedestal and an acanthus drop, the ring it hangs by). Its hands keep the real time. Land on the bench (the bench vent) and it comes alive: it slides down the wall to fight you there, its mask glaring (that's where to hit it), and goes back up when you leave. **Sweeping hands**: a half circle fills on the bench while the hands whirl, then a golden hand sweeps right across it for 3: jump it, or stand in a far front corner of the bench, out of its reach. **Victory's wreath**: she raises the wreath while a line on the bench shows where she'll throw it (it locks halfway), then it flies out along it and back, 2 each way. Under half health it also **strikes the hour**: three chimes, each a golden ring rolling out across the bench (jump each one). Beat it and it goes back up its wall as a plain clock. In act 1 it hangs there as decoration. Tucked into the hall's far corner beside the front door stands a third: **the Cream Whipper**, a black N2O cylinder (brass valve, chrome regulator with two pressure gauges whose needles swing as it works) hosed to a brushed-steel cream whipper. Its **balloon**: it blows a balloon up on the whipper's nozzle (a hiss, the gauges climbing), lets it go, and the balloon (about 18 cm tall, bigger than you) drifts after you, slower than you swim, its knot just over your head, with a violet circle on the floor showing how far its burst reaches, ticking and blinking faster; 3 s after it's let go it bursts. Caught in the circle: 2, and all the sound (music and effects) goes deep and muffled for 5 s, then eases back to normal over 3 more. Its **cream spray**: it tips toward you while a cone fills on the floor, then sprays whipped cream along it (2, and it slows you). Under half health it blows two balloons at a time and sprays longer. The tub is dressed after the real one (`src/bathroom.js`, decoration only): white stacked-subway tile, a pink-and-white striped non-slip mat and a domed steel drain stopper, brushed-steel spout, thermostat plate and shower slide rail with its hand shower and hose, the overflow plate and grey hose over the rim, a smiley scrub sponge and two Pantene bottles on the rim, and a black wire caddy on the back wall with its bottles and a loofah. When time runs out **the Clog** rises from the bathtub drain and you fight it in the tub: a huge wet matted hairball (hundreds of fine strands in mixed browns with a grey and a blonde one, a pink hair tie, a bobby pin, soap scum, a cotton bud and a bead caught in it, slime dripping off it, glowing eyes and a toothy maw peering through a parting in the hair, four thick hair ropes). Like the Vacuum, it rolls after you and bumps you back, and between chases cycles through telegraphed attacks: **hair lash** (a rope rears up while a line fills on the tub floor, the aim locking halfway, then whips down along it: 3 and tangled, slowed), **snare** (three circles fill round you, then tufts of hair spring up in them: 2 and slowed), **roll** (it spins up while its lane lights up, then rolls down it: 4), **fill and drain** (the spout gushes and the tub fills over 2 s while a ring fills round the drain; then the plug pulls, the water drains in 3 s and a whirlpool drags you to the drain and the hairball sitting on it: swim hard away) and **shed** (it shakes off hair tangles). The first time it drops below 70% it roars, **four ladybugs burst out of its hair**, and it sinks into the drain: the bar says IN THE DRAIN and it can't be hurt until they're dead (30 s at most). That happens once. Below 45% it's angry: faster, shorter pauses, double lashes, and a spinning **gunk spray** in three streams at once. Its warnings are a sickly yellow-green. (`src/clog.js`.) Beat it and the run ends (act 3, the bedroom, is next). The act lives in `src/stage2.js`; `src/stages.js` lists the acts and carries the run between them (sessionStorage, reloading the page). The dev tools' **🚪 Other act** jumps between act 1 and act 2 with a fresh run.

**Music:** an adaptive soundtrack synthesized in the browser (`src/music.js`), in the spirit of Risk of Rain 2. Exploring plays "Puddle Drift", inspired by Daft Punk's Tron: Legacy score: dark string pads, then a pulsing sequenced analog bass that carries the song, a slow arpeggio, deep half-time drums and toms, and a low brass lead as more bugs close in and the night gets later, with no bright highs to distract; the Vacuum fight plays "Domestic Machinery", after deadmau5's "HR 8938 Cephei" but dubbier: pumping supersaw chords, a wobbling reese bass that speeds up as the Vacuum weakens, risers into every phrase, and a supersaw lead once it's below about 60% health; its suction drags the whole mix through a dub filter. The Vacuum has its own sounds for everything it does: a boot-up chime, an alarm and a roar for the charge and a clanging crash into the wall, a wobbling dub roar for the suction, whirring brushes and swishing sweeps, a clunk and skittering for the roach dump, a whirling siren for the spin, a wail and four pops for the lanternflies, a shimmering shield that pings when hit and shatters, an angry siren as it enrages at half health, and a long power-down when it dies. The pause menu muffles it; 🎵 turns it off (M mutes everything).

**Getting around:** the jelly swims at a steady speed while its bell pulses in strokes (`src/swim.js`), faster the harder you push. It has a single jump, 20 cm high (`jumpHeight` in `src/config.js`: enough to jump out of the bathroom sink bowl, 17.5 cm deep); each Pen Spring treasure adds a jump in mid-air (take up to 3 for a quadruple jump). Floor vents fling you in an arc straight onto the sofa, coffee table, media console, desk, lounge chair and stovetop. Hold Space to climb the front of the corduroy sofa.

## Changing the game

Most changes are edits to plain-text files, not code. `CLAUDE.md` is the full map (agents read it automatically):

- **`content/enemies.kdl`**: every bug as a few numbers and a few behaviour words, e.g. `chase 0.3`, or `curl-dash windup=0.6 time=0.7 speed=0.75 rest=0.8 dmg=3`. Each word is defined once in `src/words.js` with what its numbers mean. A typo stops loading with a message naming the file and line.
- **`content/waves.kdl`**: how fast bugs arrive, how many at once (living bugs only: the elites and the boss don't take a place), how much tougher they get, and from when each kind appears.
- **`content/treasures.kdl`**: all 102 treasures, each a name, icon, text and effect words (and `stack=N` for stackable ones, or `level 2 text="…" { … }` / `level 3 …` blocks for ones that level up), e.g. `pierce 3`, `crit chance=0.2 mult=3`, or `every 5 { ring 0.13 dmg=1 push=0.035 }`. A new treasure that combines existing effects needs no code.
- **`content/look.css`**: colours and render numbers: the night fill, haze, bloom, the jelly's size, colour, glow and light, how much enemies glow, gut colours, the camera, and how often and how sharp the game draws (frame-rate cap, pixel ratio range, how many lamps a phone lights at once).

**Stories** (`src/stories.js`) are named situations in the real game, like Storybook: `index.html?story=words/curl-dash` opens one live (on a phone too), `index.html?stories` lists them all. Each story's `play()` also runs headlessly and checks what should happen. `node tests/run.mjs` plays every story plus a phone touch check and fails on any broken behaviour or console error. Setup: `cd tests && npm install && npx playwright install chromium`.

**The balance bot** (`node tests/balance.mjs 5`) plays whole nights on autopilot and reports how long it lasted, its level and kills, whether it beat the boss, and which sources took its Health. Run it before and after anything that could make the game harder or easier.

**The matchup test** (`BUGS=roach node tests/matchup.mjs`) puts every treasure (at level 1 and its top level), every Epic level-up card and every evolution against one big bug and a chasing clump of ten, and reports how much faster each clears them than nothing does. Leave out `BUGS=` to try every bug. Use it to spot outliers; rate a single treasure with `tests/clear.mjs`.

## Dev: layout editor

**Dev tools** sit behind one **🔒 Dev** button on the main screen: the password (`chan`, in `src/main.js`; it keeps the tools out of players' way, it isn't security) shows 🛠 Layout, 👹 Fight boss, 🐞 1 on 1, 🚪 Other act and 🩺 Diagnostics. They stay open for the tab (sessionStorage).

**👹 Fight boss** starts a fresh run and goes straight to the boss (level 1, no treasures), for testing the fight.

**🧪 Test kit** (`src/test-kit.js`) tries any treasure or evolution on the run in front of you: search or filter by rarity, **+** adds a copy (or the next level, or an evolution), **−** takes one away, and the row up top shows what you have. **New test run** starts a clean one with the waves off and you unable to die (both are toggles); **Spawn 10 roaches** or **a mixed pack** of this act's bugs puts something in front of you to try it on. The game holds still while it's open; **▶ Play with this** carries on.

**🐞 1 on 1** lists every enemy (each bug, plus every act's elites; picking an elite from another act reloads the game into that act first): pick one and a fresh run starts with just it, no waves, scheduled treasures or boss. A bug turns up near you and another comes 1.5 s after you clear it; an elite puts you on its high ground and comes back 4 s after you beat it. Pause to pick another; Restart goes back to a normal run (`Run.startDuel` in `src/run.js`).

**🛠 Layout** (dev tools) lets you move, rotate, raise/lower and hide the furniture and objects. Tap or click an object to select it (a yellow box shows it), drag it to move it. Desktop: WASD pan, wheel zoom, right-drag turns the view, Q/E rotate 15° (Shift: 90°), R/F raise/lower 1 cm (Shift: 5 cm), H hide, Ctrl+Z undo. Touch: drag empty space to pan, pinch to zoom, and use the toolbar.

Edits are saved in the browser (`localStorage` key `jf-layout-v1`) and applied on every load before collisions are built, so moved furniture is solid where it is. **Export** shows a JSON code; paste it back to Claude to bake the layout into the stage for everyone. Baked edits live in `src/layout-baked.js`; they load first and become each object's "home", with your own browser edits on top. **Vents** are edited here too: each shows an orange disc (the grille on the floor) and a cyan ball (where it throws you) joined by the jump's arc. Drag either and it sticks to whatever surface is under the pointer (point at a cushion or a shelf); R/F nudge it up or down, ⌂ puts it back. They export with the furniture as `vent:<name>` entries, which get baked into the vent list in `src/stage1.js`. Other stage markers (treasure spots, elites) don't follow moved furniture yet. Code: `src/layout.js`.

## Hosting on Cloudflare (public leaderboard)

The game also runs as a Cloudflare Worker named `jelly-fight` (`wrangler.jsonc`). The Worker serves the game's files from Cloudflare's CDN and answers `api/scores` (`worker/index.js`) from a D1 database called `jelly-fight-scores`, so anyone can post a score: signed in or not, any organization. On the game's own site, `src/leaderboard.js` uses that; on claude.ai it still uses the artifact's database.

- **Deploys:** Cloudflare Workers Builds, connected to this GitHub repo (`Chanksyou/Jellyfight`; the Cloudflare GitHub app needs access to it). Don't use the dashboard's "Import a repository": it makes a separate copy that never gets updates. Every push to `main` redeploys. Build command: none. Deploy command: `npx wrangler deploy`. Wrangler creates the D1 database on the first deploy (no ID to paste), and the Worker creates its tables on the first request.
- **What's served:** the repo root, minus what `.assetsignore` lists (`tests/`, `tools/`, `worker/`, the config, the docs, and the unused whole-apartment models).
- **The board's rules:** a player is a random id kept in their browser, and the board only ever sends back your own (an id is all it takes to post as that player). Scores are capped at 60,000, names are trimmed to 20 characters with HTML stripped, and each player can post once every 15 s and each address once every 5 s (addresses are stored only as a salted hash; set an `IP_SALT` secret on the Worker to choose your own salt). Scores come from the player's browser, so this stops casual cheating, not a determined cheater.
- **Try it locally:** `npx wrangler dev`, then open http://localhost:8787.

## How it fits together

The apartment is a static 3D model, baked to `assets/apartment.glb` (geometry, materials, textures, and doors as separate nodes), plus `assets/apartment.json` (floor plan, acts, door angles, the lights at midnight). The game doesn't load that whole file: `tools/split.mjs` cuts it into one file per act, and each act loads only its own rooms. `apartment-act1.glb` (~3.6 MB) is the living room and the hallway you can see down from it (seen only: an invisible wall keeps you out of it in act 1), plus every room's walls, floors and doors, so every view is solid; `apartment-act2.glb` (~1.1 MB) the bathroom and hall closets; `apartment-act3.glb` (~0.9 MB) the bedroom. A stage lists the parts it needs (`parts` in `src/stage1.js`); `GAME.loadRooms(['act2'])` brings in more at the start of a later act (solid, with layout edits, on the minimap, with that act's lamps). Each part is meshopt-compressed (needs WebAssembly) with a `-q` twin used automatically if WebAssembly is blocked. Nothing from the original apartment app runs in the game.

`src/boot.js` sets up three.js (r186 from jsDelivr), loads the apartment with `src/apartment.js`, then starts `src/main.js`.

The apartment's colors and lights were authored for three.js r128, so the game keeps color management off and `src/legacy-lighting.js` renders the lights with r128's falloff and scaling.

The camera can sit 2 mm from a wall without flicker thanks to a reversed depth buffer (`src/boot.js`). Where the GPU lacks it, desktop falls back to a logarithmic depth buffer and phones (log depth defeats their GPUs' hidden-surface removal) to a 5 mm near plane.

### Re-baking the apartment

`tools/apartment-source.html` is the full apartment app, kept only as the source for the bake. After changing it:

```
cd tools
npm install
npx playwright install chromium
npm run export
```

The bathroom's close-up details live in the source too: the vanity's undermount basin (a real bowl under a hole in the counter), the oval toilet seat, the loop-pile bath mat, canvas laundry bags, panelled doors and fabric coats. A material whose bump map is its own color map keeps its bump through the bake.

The exporter (`tools/exporter.js`) keeps the meshes inside the rooms. It drops everything else: the neighborhood, the corridor, the sky, Dendi, particles and helpers. Doors are exported closed as `door-<id>` nodes. `tools/compress.mjs` then meshopt-compresses the geometry (about 17 MB down to 5 MB).

## Characters and swapping in a real model

A look is plain JSON (`{ name, body, finish, color, accent, pattern, eyes, eyeColor, mouth, top, topColor, feet, size }`). `buildCharacter(look, heightMeters)` returns an avatar:

```js
{ root: THREE.Object3D, update(dt, { speed, walkSpeed, grounded, vy, swim, vel }), land(impact), dispose(),
  pulse(), tentacles }   // optional: a jump's bell squeeze; a TentacleRig the Lash strikes with
```

The player only talks to that interface (`player.setAvatar(avatar)`), so a rigged glTF character can replace the procedural one by writing another builder that returns the same shape. It would drive its animation mixer from `update()` and map the look's colors and hat onto the model.
