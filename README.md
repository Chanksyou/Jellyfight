# Jelly Fight

A third-person game where you play a ~9 cm jellyfish of your own design inside a real-scale 3D model of the apartment.

## Run it

ES modules won't load over `file://`, so serve the folder:

```
npx http-server -c-1        # or: python -m http.server
```

Open the printed localhost URL. The VS Code "Live Server" extension also works.

## The game

You're an immortal jellyfish growing up over one night in the apartment. Stage 1 (Polyp) is the living room and its kitchen strip, midnight to 2 am (5 real minutes).

It's night: the room is lit only by its lamps (warm pools of light, dark corners, a faint haze across the room), and you're a fluorescent jelly in it: a tall clear bell with glowing canals and a bright rim, long glowing tentacles and frilly blue oral arms, with a soft halo and a small light of your own that tints the floor around you. The bugs, elites and the Vacuum carry a faint glow and a warm rim light so they read against the dark, and their angry eyes shine. That's act 1; act 2 is the hallway and the bathroom (below).

- **You attack on your own, two ways.** Your main attack is **bubbles**: the bell blows a steady stream of bubbles, one after another (about 1.8 a second to start), at the nearest enemy in range; each pops hard (6 damage) with a small splash. Blow-rate cards make the stream faster. Each extra bubble (a +1 Bubble card, Bubble Wand, Ephyra Bell) adds one more bubble to every blow, side by side and flying parallel, but costs damage: every bubble does 15% less per extra one, compounding (2 bubbles do 85% each, 3 do 72%, 6 do 44%), so more bubbles cover a wider lane for less punch each. Level-up cards improve them (bubbles, range, pop damage, blow rate). Element treasures infuse them, and they stack: fire (burns; shatters frozen enemies), lightning (arcs to 2 more), ice (chills, then freezes), acid (puddles), wind (knockback) and glitter (bigger splash). Each element changes the projectile itself so you can see it coming: a fireball trailing flames, a crackling ball of lightning, a spinning ice shard, a dripping acid glob, a gust of spinning wind rings, a glitter bomb shedding sparks (with several elements, bubbles take turns). Up close, your **tentacles** sting automatically at a fixed strength; the Stinging Frills evolution is the only thing that changes them. You swim, jump and position.
- **Bugs** come for you and drop **dew** (your XP) when you clear them: **cockroaches** scuttle straight at you (a little slower than you swim, so you can always get away from one); **ant squads** (five ants in a block, from 0:30) curl into a ball and roll into you; **mosquitoes** hover out of reach and shoot laser bolts: a flickering red beam shows where the bolt will go (brighter as the shot nears), it locks on halfway through, and stepping off the line dodges it. From 2:00, **spotted lanternflies** crawl after you, crouch over a red ring marking where they'll land, then leap high (flashing their red hindwings) and slam down on it: step out of the ring. There aren't many at once, but each is worth a lot. In act 2, **giant house spiders** (from 0:45) stalk after you on eight long, hairy legs (a real spider's gait: two alternating sets of four) but keep their distance, stopping about 55 cm away and backing off if you close in; from there one rears up, front legs raised, and lobs a ball of web that arcs down onto where you were: it stings (2) and webs you, slowing your swimming for 1.5 s. Keep moving and it lands behind you. Also in act 2, **house flies** (from 0:30) buzz in close beside you, hang there rubbing their front legs and draw back while a short orange line on the floor marks the strike (it locks halfway through), then poke you with their straw of a mouth for 3: step off the line or back out of reach. And from 1:00 in act 2, **giant millipedes** (glossy olive-black rings banded orange, rows of rippling orange legs) slither after you like snakes, swinging round in arcs (their bodies never fold through themselves). Up close one curls into an armoured ball standing on its edge (hits just glance off it: IMMUNE), revs up spinning in place while its lane lights up amber on the floor (the aim locks halfway through), then rolls down it at you for 4. Get out of the lane, and hit it once it uncurls. Each piece of high ground has an **elite**, an object there that came alive, each with two attacks it alternates, every one telegraphed on the ground before it lands: the **Controller** on the media console (three colored aim lines, then a button barrage; a red rumble circle that fills, then a shockwave), the **Mug** on the desk (coffee lobs onto filling brown circles that leave scalding puddles; a tip-over with an arrow, then a spill that pours puddles along it) and the **Kettle** on the stove (whistles with a blue steam cone drawn where it'll blast; boils over, raining drops on filling orange circles). They turn to face you before winding up and only fight when you come close. Beat one for 20 dew, moisture and a treasure pick.
- **HUD:** top left, your level in a gold ring, a moisture bar with its number (red when low), a thin gold XP line and your treasures. Top middle, a big countdown to the boss, with the night's clock and who's coming under it. The minimap sits bottom right.
- **Golden gifts:** at 0:15, 1:00, 1:45, 2:30, 3:00 and 3:45 a bright glowing gold gift box turns up somewhere else in the room (under a tall beam of gold light, and on the minimap) and waits 15 s, its ring on the floor draining and blinking as time runs out. Reach it in time for a treasure pick; otherwise it fades away. A gift never sits on a vent's landing point: a spot that close moves over 15-35 cm on the same surface. The times and how long each stays are `gifts` in `src/stage1.js`.
- **Every run starts with a treasure:** pick 1 of 3 before the night begins: two are elements for your bubbles (fire, lightning, ice, acid, wind or glitter) and the third is anything else. The elements are rare treasures (common for wind), tuned with `tests/clear.mjs` so each damaging one clears a pack about 2x faster, like other rare treasures; their numbers are `ELEMENT` in `src/bubbles.js`.
- **Level up** by collecting dew (cockroaches drop 1, ant squads 2, mosquitoes 5; each level needs 1.5x the dew of the last, starting at 3: 3, 5, 7, 10, 15, 23…): pick 1 of 3 cards, each one stat and one number (bubbles, range, pop damage, blow rate, max moisture, swim speed, moisture regen, dodge %, luck). Regen, dodge and luck start at 0; dodge caps at 60%. Luck makes rarer cards and treasures come up more often. One free reroll per level.
- **The minimap** is kept quiet: the room in one tone with faint footprints of the big furniture, you as a cyan arrow, small red dots for bugs, and coloured dots for what's worth going to (gold gift, orange elites, red boss), with a tiny ▲ / ▼ when one is well above or below you.
- **Treasures** come from golden gifts, beaten elites and the starting pick: pick 1 of 3 lost things to keep all run, each rated common, rare, epic or legendary (rarer ones come up less often; luck shifts the odds). Most are unique (one each); plain number boosts (Lemon Slice, Hand Cream, Odd Sock…) are stackable and can be picked up to 2 or 3 times, showing ×2 in the HUD. There are 47: bubble and element upgrades, stat boosts (regen, dodge, damage, range), Brotato-style trade-offs (a glass cannon, life steal, bait that brings more bugs and more dew) and slow growers (+max moisture or damage every N kills), things that attack on their own (a chord every 5 s, remote zaps, orbiting fairy lights, a burning moonbeam, Lego traps, bowling marbles, freezes, auras), and rule-benders (marks that make enemies take more damage, more dew, better or more level-up cards, slower bugs).
- **The Vacuum** comes when the timer runs out: a robot vacuum that powers on in front of the kitchen. It chases and bumps you, and cycles through attacks: a telegraphed charge, suction that pulls you in, spinning-brush sweeps, dumping cockroaches from its bin, launching 4 lanternflies out of its bin in high arcs (rings mark where they land) and raising a purple shield that makes it immune until all 4 are dead (tethers show which flies hold it up; the boss bar turns purple and says SHIELDED), and (once hurt) a spin that sprays dust.
- Beat it and you **metamorphose** into an Ephyra (pick 1 of 3 evolutions). Dry out and you shrink back to a polyp and start over.
- **Score and leaderboard:** every run ends with one combined score: 10 per bug cleared, +50 per golden elite bug, +300 per high-ground elite, 100 per level above 1, 150 per treasure, 2 per second survived before the boss (up to 600), up to 1,500 for damage dealt to the Vacuum, and +3,000 for beating it plus 20 for every second under 3 minutes (`Run.scoreParts` in `src/run.js`). On the game's claude.ai page the score goes on a shared leaderboard (🏆 in the pause menu and on the end screen): one entry per player, their best run, top 10 shown (`src/leaderboard.js`, the artifact's `db`). Posting needs edit access to the page; everyone signed in can see it; anywhere else the game keeps your best in the browser.
- **5 minutes per stage.** Then the boss comes: grab what gifts you can before it does.
- **Act 2: the hallway and the bathroom.** Beat the Vacuum and grow, and the end screen offers **On to act 2 →**: the page reloads into the hallway with everything the run had (level, stats, treasures, score so far, which also goes on the leaderboard). Bugs are 1.5x tougher, and the waves are act 2's own: cockroaches and **hair clumps** from the start, ant squads from 0:20, **house flies** from 0:30, **spiders** from 0:45, **millipedes** from 1:00, mosquitoes from 1:15, **staplers** from 1:45, lanternflies from 2:30. Five vents fling you onto the vanity, the toilet lid, the sideboard, the bench and the recycling bin, and golden gifts turn up in 14 spots across both rooms (bath mat, rugs, vanity, inside the sink, toilet lid, the hall runner, the sideboard, the bench…). The laundry and coat closets stand ajar so you can peek in, but a force field keeps you out (and out of the living room). The vanity has an elite: **the Soap Dispenser** (the glass pump bottle by the tap, standing in for the real one; the vanity vent lands you beside it). Its pink soap drains as you hurt it, so the level is its health. It alternates a **soap squirt** (three pump strokes, each lobbing a glob onto a pink circle filling where you stand: 2 damage, and the glob leaves a slick that takes your grip away so you slide) and a **bubble ring** (it foams up while a pink ring fills around it, then lets go a ring of soap bubbles drifting outward with one gap to slip through, 2 each). Under half health it's angry: quicker, four squirts, and a second ring right after the first with the gap elsewhere. Beat it for a treasure pick. The hallway's ornate bronze wall clock over the cubby bench (the one with the tennis ball) is an elite too: **the Wall Clock**, modelled in detail after the real one (Victory in an arched niche raising a laurel wreath and holding a herald's horn, a dial with gilt Roman numerals and pierced gilt hands in a laurel garland, a bearded mask with acanthus wings, a lattice pedestal and an acanthus drop, the ring it hangs by). Its hands keep the real time. Land on the bench (the bench vent) and it comes alive: it slides down the wall to fight you there, its mask glaring (that's where to hit it), and goes back up when you leave. **Sweeping hands**: a half circle fills on the bench while the hands whirl, then a golden hand sweeps right across it for 3: jump it, or stand in a far front corner of the bench, out of its reach. **Victory's wreath**: she raises the wreath while a line on the bench shows where she'll throw it (it locks halfway), then it flies out along it and back, 2 each way. Under half health it also **strikes the hour**: three chimes, each a golden ring rolling out across the bench (jump each one). Beat it and it goes back up its wall as a plain clock. In act 1 it hangs there as decoration. By the front door's corner stands a third: **the Cream Whipper**, a black N2O cylinder (brass valve, chrome regulator with two pressure gauges whose needles swing as it works) hosed to a brushed-steel cream whipper. Its **balloon**: it blows a balloon up on the whipper's nozzle (a hiss, the gauges climbing), lets it go, and the balloon drifts after you, slower than you swim, sinking to your height, with a violet circle on the floor showing how far its burst reaches, ticking and blinking faster; 3 s after it's let go it bursts. Caught in the circle: 2, and all the sound (music and effects) goes deep and muffled for 5 s, then eases back to normal over 3 more. Its **cream spray**: it tips toward you while a cone fills on the floor, then sprays whipped cream along it (2, and it slows you). Under half health it blows two balloons at a time and sprays longer. When time runs out **the Clog**, a hairball, rises from the bathtub drain and you fight it in the tub. Beat it and the run ends (act 3, the bedroom, is next). The act lives in `src/stage2.js`; `src/stages.js` lists the acts and carries the run between them (sessionStorage, reloading the page). The pause menu's **🚪 Other act (dev)** jumps between act 1 and act 2 with a fresh run.

**Music:** an adaptive soundtrack synthesized in the browser (`src/music.js`), in the spirit of Risk of Rain 2. Exploring plays "Puddle Drift", inspired by Daft Punk's Tron: Legacy score: dark string pads, then a pulsing sequenced analog bass that carries the song, a slow arpeggio, deep half-time drums and toms, and a low brass lead as more bugs close in and the night gets later, with no bright highs to distract; the Vacuum fight plays "Domestic Machinery", after deadmau5's "HR 8938 Cephei" but dubbier: pumping supersaw chords, a wobbling reese bass that speeds up as the Vacuum weakens, risers into every phrase, and a supersaw lead once it's below about 60% health; its suction drags the whole mix through a dub filter. The Vacuum has its own sounds for everything it does: a boot-up chime, an alarm and a roar for the charge and a clanging crash into the wall, a wobbling dub roar for the suction, whirring brushes and swishing sweeps, a clunk and skittering for the roach dump, a whirling siren for the spin, a wail and four pops for the lanternflies, a shimmering shield that pings when hit and shatters, an angry siren below 45%, and a long power-down when it dies. The pause menu muffles it; 🎵 turns it off (M mutes everything).

**Getting around:** the jelly swims at a steady speed while its bell pulses in strokes (`src/swim.js`), faster the harder you push. It has a single jump; each Pen Spring treasure adds a jump in mid-air (take up to 3 for a quadruple jump). Floor vents fling you in an arc straight onto the sofa, coffee table, media console, desk, lounge chair and stovetop. Hold Space to climb the front of the corduroy sofa.

## Changing the game

Most changes are edits to plain-text files, not code. `CLAUDE.md` is the full map (agents read it automatically):

- **`content/enemies.kdl`**: every bug as a few numbers and a few behaviour words, e.g. `chase 0.3`, or `curl-dash windup=0.6 time=0.7 speed=0.75 rest=0.8 dmg=3`. Each word is defined once in `src/words.js` with what its numbers mean. A typo stops loading with a message naming the file and line.
- **`content/waves.kdl`**: how fast bugs arrive, how many at once, how much tougher they get, and from when each kind appears.
- **`content/treasures.kdl`**: all 47 treasures, each a name, icon, text and effect words (and `stack=N` for stackable ones), e.g. `pierce 3`, `crit chance=0.2 mult=3`, or `every 5 { ring 0.13 dmg=1.2 push=0.035 }`. A new treasure that combines existing effects needs no code.
- **`content/look.css`**: colours and render numbers: the night fill, haze, bloom, the jelly's size, colour, glow and light, how much enemies glow, gut colours, the camera.

**Stories** (`src/stories.js`) are named situations in the real game, like Storybook: `index.html?story=words/curl-dash` opens one live (on a phone too), `index.html?stories` lists them all. Each story's `play()` also runs headlessly and checks what should happen. `node tests/run.mjs` plays every story plus a phone touch check and fails on any broken behaviour or console error. Setup: `cd tests && npm install && npx playwright install chromium`.

**The balance bot** (`node tests/balance.mjs 5`) plays whole nights on autopilot and reports how long it lasted, its level and kills, whether it beat the boss, and which sources took its moisture. Run it before and after anything that could make the game harder or easier.

## Dev: layout editor

**👹 Fight boss (dev)** in the pause menu starts a fresh run and goes straight to the boss (level 1, no treasures), for testing the fight.

**🐞 1 on 1 (dev)** lists every enemy (each bug, plus every act's elites; picking an elite from another act reloads the game into that act first): pick one and a fresh run starts with just it, no waves, gifts or boss. A bug turns up near you and another comes 1.5 s after you clear it; an elite puts you on its high ground and comes back 4 s after you beat it. Pause to pick another; Restart goes back to a normal run (`Run.startDuel` in `src/run.js`).

**🛠 Layout (dev)** in the pause menu lets you move, rotate, raise/lower and hide the furniture and objects. Tap or click an object to select it (a yellow box shows it), drag it to move it. Desktop: WASD pan, wheel zoom, right-drag turns the view, Q/E rotate 15° (Shift: 90°), R/F raise/lower 1 cm (Shift: 5 cm), H hide, Ctrl+Z undo. Touch: drag empty space to pan, pinch to zoom, and use the toolbar.

Edits are saved in the browser (`localStorage` key `jf-layout-v1`) and applied on every load before collisions are built, so moved furniture is solid where it is. **Export** shows a JSON code; paste it back to Claude to bake the layout into the stage for everyone. Baked edits live in `src/layout-baked.js`; they load first and become each object's "home", with your own browser edits on top. **Vents** are edited here too: each shows an orange disc (the grille on the floor) and a cyan ball (where it throws you) joined by the jump's arc. Drag either and it sticks to whatever surface is under the pointer (point at a cushion or a shelf); R/F nudge it up or down, ⌂ puts it back. They export with the furniture as `vent:<name>` entries, which get baked into the vent list in `src/stage1.js`. Other stage markers (golden gift spots, elites) don't follow moved furniture yet. Code: `src/layout.js`.

## Controls

WASD move · mouse look · Space jump (hold to climb fabric; again in the air with Pen Spring) · 1/2/3 pick a card · R reroll · wheel zoom · Esc pause · F3 debug readout. "Look" in the pause menu opens the jellyfish creator: pick a species (Sea Nettle, Moon Jelly, Lion's Mane, Box Jelly, Crystal Jelly, Fried Egg), then its colours, eyes (dots, big, sleepy, fierce or cyclops: flat on the bell, sized to fit each species), mouth and something on top (16 hats and toppers: antennae, sprout, party hat, bow, crown, horns, top hat, beanie, witch hat, cowboy hat, a spinning halo, flower, propeller cap with a spinning propeller, chef hat, cat ears, unicorn horn). Every jelly glows and is the same size. On phones: left thumb moves, right thumb looks, ⤴ jumps (hold to climb; tap again in the air with Pen Spring).

## How it fits together

The apartment is a static 3D model, baked to `assets/apartment.glb` (geometry, materials, textures, and doors as separate nodes), plus `assets/apartment.json` (floor plan, acts, door angles, the lights at midnight). The game doesn't load that whole file: `tools/split.mjs` cuts it into one file per act, and each act loads only its own rooms. `apartment-act1.glb` (~3.6 MB) is the living room and the hallway you can see down from it (seen only: an invisible wall keeps you out of it in act 1), plus every room's walls, floors and doors, so every view is solid; `apartment-act2.glb` (~1.1 MB) the bathroom and hall closets; `apartment-act3.glb` (~0.9 MB) the bedroom. A stage lists the parts it needs (`parts` in `src/stage1.js`); `GAME.loadRooms(['act2'])` brings in more at the start of a later act (solid, with layout edits, on the minimap, with that act's lamps). Each part is meshopt-compressed (needs WebAssembly) with a `-q` twin used automatically if WebAssembly is blocked. Nothing from the original apartment app runs in the game.

`src/boot.js` sets up three.js (r170 from jsDelivr), loads the apartment with `src/apartment.js`, then starts `src/main.js`.

The apartment's colors and lights were authored for three.js r128, so the game keeps color management off and `src/legacy-lighting.js` renders the lights with r128's falloff and scaling.

Desktop uses a logarithmic depth buffer, so the camera can sit 2 mm from a wall without flicker. Phones skip it (it defeats their GPUs' hidden-surface removal) and use a 5 mm near plane.

### Re-baking the apartment

`tools/apartment-source.html` is the full apartment app, kept only as the source for the bake. After changing it:

```
cd tools
npm install
npx playwright install chromium
npm run export
```

The bathroom's close-up details live in the source too: the vanity's undermount basin (a real bowl under a hole in the counter), the oval toilet seat, the loop-pile bath mat, canvas laundry bags, panelled doors and fabric coats. A material whose bump map is its own color map keeps its bump through the bake.

The exporter (`tools/exporter.js`) keeps the meshes inside the rooms. It drops everything else: the neighborhood, the corridor, the sky, Dendi, particles and helpers. Doors are exported closed as `door-<id>` nodes. `tools/compress.mjs` then meshopt-compresses the geometry (about 17 MB down to 5 MB).

## Characters and swapping in a real model

A look is plain JSON (`{ name, body, finish, color, accent, pattern, eyes, eyeColor, mouth, top, topColor, feet, size }`). `buildCharacter(look, heightMeters)` returns an avatar:

```js
{ root: THREE.Object3D, update(dt, { speed, walkSpeed, grounded, vy, swim, vel }), land(impact), dispose(),
  pulse(), tentacles }   // optional: a jump's bell squeeze; a TentacleRig the Lash strikes with
```

The player only talks to that interface (`player.setAvatar(avatar)`), so a rigged glTF character can replace the procedural one by writing another builder that returns the same shape. It would drive its animation mixer from `update()` and map the look's colors and hat onto the model.
